## What this is

A reference of frameworks and tools, drawn from the [UN Inter-Agency Career Week 2026](https://learning.unog.ch/interagency-career-week-2026) sessions, organised by a [model](career-navigation-model) of seven dimensions. Every page is self-contained, so you can land on a single one without reading the rest.

## What this is not

The official record of IACW 2026. The Centre for Learning and Multilingualism at the UN Office at Geneva owns that record; recordings will be posted on the [event's homepage](https://learning.unog.ch/interagency-career-week-2026) as the organisers release them.

## Why it exists

The five days of IACW 2026 contained substantial practical guidance. The volume and fragmentation of the material made it hard to absorb and reuse in the moment of need. The toolkit organises it.


## What is missing

The [Event Coverage Analysis](agenda-coverage-audit) shows two patterns in the event, both pointing in the same direction: 
1. the **topical coverage was uneven** across the seven dimensions of the model (heavy on inner work, narrative, and application mechanics; thin on the operational middle), and
2. the **speaker mix was overwhelmingly the system speaking**, not the candidate. The toolkit is built on top of what the event offered. Two parallel efforts try to fill what was missing.


## What is happening alongside the toolkit

Two initiatives aimed at filling the gaps:

> **A small set of short career conversations**, with professionals working across international development, humanitarian, and adjacent sectors. The goal is not formal research, but a clearer sense of where people experience the greatest friction, how they navigate career uncertainty, which forms of support are genuinely useful, how they interpret their transferable skills, and where current career-guidance ecosystems may still leave gaps. The conversations help surface patterns that may not appear clearly in more institutionally framed discussions about careers.

> **A working prototype of an AI tool to identify job opportunities relevant to you** A tool that uses AI to address one specific dimension of the navigation problem: helping people look beyond the pool of organisations they already know, understand how their skills translate into adjacent organisations and sectors, and identify which specific job openings correspond to their experience even when described in unfamiliar language. 
> The tool that reads what you have done, reads how organisations describe what they need, and surfaces the roles where the match is genuine even when the language is not. I built it for myself first. It is working for me, and has surfaced roles I would not have found on my own. A small number of people are starting to test it.

The **toolkit**, the **conversation**, and the **protoype** are three separate pieces. They share the same observation about where the navigation problem actually lives.

## Who is behind this

I am Davide Piga, a Knowledge and Innovation Advisor with nearly two decades of experience working across international organisations including several UN entities and the World Bank. Much of my work over the years has focused on how knowledge moves inside organisations: how expertise is surfaced, how people learn from one another, and how information becomes practical and reusable instead of fragmented and difficult to access. 

More recently, my work has increasingly explored the intersection between collective intelligence, AI, knowledge work, and professional development, particularly in contexts where people and organisations are navigating complexity, uncertainty, and transition.

This toolkit sits at the intersection of those interests.
